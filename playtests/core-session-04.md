# AX&X Solo Playtest, Session 04

A controlled follow-up to Session 02, designed to isolate one variable. It replays
**Session 02's exact party, classes, kindred, and gold**, with one change: the
ability scores of Orsible (Magic-User) and Branna (Cleric) are **swapped**, so each
caster has the prime requisite its class actually wants. Session 02 note 1 found
that the roll pointed Orsible at a Cleric and Branna at a Mage; this run tests what
happens when that mistake is undone, holding everything else equal.

What is held the same as Session 02: the four archetypes (Fighter, Cleric, Thief,
Magic-User), so the party still has a single front-line tank and a soft Thief, not
Session 03's two Warriors. What differs: the two casters now work, the current
softened Brush table is in force, and monster targeting is played as a neutral GM.

Same dungeon, same four encounters, same monster HP (ghoul 10; orcs 9/5/5;
skeletons 2/6/5; Warden 40). One continuous delve, no rest. Dice quoted inline as
`[dice] = total`.

## The Party

Mod table: 3 = -3, 4-5 = -2, 6-8 = -1, 9-12 = 0, 13-15 = +1, 16-17 = +2, 18 = +3.

- **Slots: 2.** INT 13 is +1, which grants the bonus Magic-User slot. Spellbook and prepared: Magic Missile and Sleep. In Session 02 this character had INT 10 and a single slot; now he is a real caster.
- Saves: Body 0, Dodge 0, Will 0. Gold 90.

### Branna, Dwarf Cleric (Lawful) — recalculated with Orsible's rolls

Party HP 23, the same as Session 02, and the same shape: one tank, one soft Thief,
two casters. The only real difference walking in is that the casters function.

## First Encounter: The Ghoul

Ghoul, HP 10, AC 14, +2, claw/claw/bite (1d3), any hit Body save vs 12 or Paralyzed,
Mindless.

- **Surprise:** neither (party `[6]`, ghoul `[5]`). **Init** party `[5]` vs ghoul `[6]`, ghoul first.
- The ghoul hits Hurgo (AC 15) twice: claw `[17]`+2 = 19, claw `[11]` miss, bite `[18]`+2 = 20, for 1 and 2 (Hurgo 8 -> 5). A hit forces a Body save: Hurgo `[19]`+2 = 21 vs 12, made easily.
- Party: Hurgo `[18]`+1 hits for 5 (ghoul 10 -> 5), Branna and Pip miss, **Orsible's Magic Missile** auto-hits for 5. **Ghoul dead in round 1.**

Hurgo binds wounds afterward (1d4 = `[4]`) back to full. Where Session 02's ghoul got
a free surprise round and paralyzed the tank, here no side was surprised, Hurgo made
his save, and Magic Missile sealed it in one round.

## Second Encounter: The Orc Patrol

Three orcs, 9/5/5, AC 13, +1 (1d8).

- **Surprise: party `[5]`, orcs `[2]` — the orcs are caught off guard and the party gets the free round.** Reaction `[1 6]` - 1 = 6 (wary), moot under surprise.
- **Orsible casts Sleep in the free round: 2d4 = `[2 1]` = 3 HD, exactly covering all three 1-HD orcs.** They drop before acting. No attacks, no damage.

The party binds the sleeping orcs and takes the chamber and its **600 GP** cache.
This is the spot that killed Hurgo in Session 02, where the Magic-User had rolled
INT 10 and carried no Sleep. With the swap, the fight simply ends. Both of Orsible's
slots are now spent.

## Third Encounter: The Skeleton Guards

Three skeletons, 2/6/5, AC 13, +1 (1d6), Mindless, vulnerable to blunt.

- **Surprise: party `[3]`, skeletons `[2]` — the skeletons are caught off guard, party free round.**
- **Branna's Turn succeeds** (`[14]`+3 = 17 vs 11), and 2d6 = `[6 2]` = 8 HD routs all three (3 HD). **The skeletons flee; no fight, no damage.**

Her WIS-16 Turn at +3 needed an 8; the Session 02 Branna at +1 would have needed a
10. The party walks through the vault unopposed. Branna's Turn refreshes for the next
fight, and both her spell slots are still in hand.

## Fourth Encounter: The Drowned Warden

HD 5, HP 40, AC 15, two slams +5 (1d8), vulnerable to fire, undead and Turnable, the
telegraphed Undertow.

- **Surprise:** neither (both `[3]`). **Init: party `[6]` vs warden `[4]`, party first.**
- **Branna's Turn on the boss fails** (`[8]`+3 = 11 vs 15; needed a 12). Her once-per-fight Turn is spent.

With the Turn gone, the boss a 40 HP wall, and the party deep on single-digit damage
per round, there is no winning this fight any more than in Session 03. But the party
won initiative and had not yet closed. Holding 600 GP, everyone alive, and the
skeletons already routed, they spend the won round withdrawing. The core has no
opportunity attacks and the Warden is bound to its vault, so they pull out before it
can reach them. **Clean retreat, no damage, boss and reliquary left behind.**

## Outcome and Aftermath

- **All four alive and at full HP.** The only damage taken all delve was 3 points on Hurgo in the ghoul fight, since healed.
- Loot: the **600 GP** orc cache and the prisoners. The skeletons were driven off; the Warden survives; the reliquary is not taken.
- XP: ghoul 50, depth 100, orcs 75, skeletons 75, boss 0, total 300, plus 600 gold. About 900 banked among four whole characters.

Same dungeon and monster HP across four runs now:

- **Session 01:** all cleared, one maiming in the boss, ~1,400 XP. (Older AC, pre-reorder Brush.)
- **Session 02:** two dead, routed before the boss, ~225 XP. (Current AC, old Brush, mismatched casters, aggressive GM.)
- **Session 03:** no deaths, one grievous, chose to retreat from the boss, ~900 XP. (Softened Brush, roll-first party with two Warriors, neutral GM.)
- **Session 04:** no deaths, no lasting wounds, clean retreat from the boss, ~900 XP. (Softened Brush, Session 02's one-tank party but with working casters, neutral GM.)

## Playtest Notes

1. **The casters were the story of Session 02, not the party shape.** This run kept
   Session 02's squishy composition exactly, one tank, a soft Thief, two casters,
   and changed only the two casters' stats so their classes functioned. The result
   went from two dead to no one even wounded past the first fight. Sleep ended the
   orcs with zero risk, and a +3 Turn routed the skeletons outright. The conclusion
   from Session 02's notes is confirmed: the disaster there was driven mainly by a
   one-slot Magic-User and a +1 Cleric, not by the lack of a second front-liner.

2. **The attribute swap is the whole difference, and it is large.** A Magic-User
   with INT 13 has two slots instead of one, which is the difference between
   carrying both Magic Missile and Sleep and carrying neither when it counts. A
   Cleric with WIS 16 Turns at +3 instead of +1 and gets a second slot and a +4
   Will save. Two prime-requisite points reshaped two entire encounters. This is the
   strongest possible argument for rolling first and assigning classes to fit,
   since the same four rolls under the right classes produce a competent party and
   under the wrong ones produce a near-TPK.

3. **The softened Brush table never triggered, so this run does not test it.** No
   character hit 0 HP. The improvement over Session 02 here is entirely the casters
   and the neutral GM, not the table. The table's effect was isolated in Session 03
   (three drops, no deaths); its non-appearance here is just how little damage a
   working caster party took.

4. **The boss is still a wall, independent of party quality.** Two good parties in a
   row (Sessions 03 and 04) reached the Drowned Warden and neither could win it; both
   retreated. The 40 HP and two +5 slams against single-digit party damage is the
   constant. If that fight is meant to be winnable at level 1, it needs less HP or
   the party needs more reliable damage; if it is meant to be a flee-or-die wall, it
   works, and winning initiative (as here) is what lets a party leave cleanly.

5. **The Thief stayed the weak link and contributed almost nothing.** Pip slung
   stones, missed most of them, had no front-line value, and faced no locks or traps
   to justify the role. The casters carried every encounter. An honest reading is
   that in this dungeon the Thief slot is close to dead weight, which is less a rules
   problem than a scenario that never asks a Thief to do its job.

6. **Winning the opening roll decides the soft encounters.** The party surprised the
   orcs and the skeletons and won the boss initiative, and each let a caster act
   before the enemy could. Against groups that Sleep or Turn can erase, acting first
   is often the whole fight. The run was lucky on these rolls; a lost surprise
   against the orcs would have let them swing before the Sleep landed, as happened on
   the tied init in Session 03.
