# AX&X Solo Playtest: The Clear-Out Cup

A rules test for a non-combat contest, built to lean on **Constitution** and the
save math rather than attacks. The Gilded Sow is clearing old stock with a drinking
contest: most empty cups in front of you at the last bell wins. A spill caught by
the house costs **3 cups**. Roughly **one cup in three has gone off**, telegraphed by
a sour reek and an oily sheen. The question this run asks: does a Body-save ladder
make CON matter, resolve on its own, and still leave room for a clever low-CON
character to compete?

## The rulings under test

Settled before the run, flagged here so the log can be read against them.

- **The drink.** Drinking a cup is a **Body save** vs 12, **minus 1 per cup already in
  front of you** (the drink piling up). Pass: nothing but merrier. Fail: one stack of
  **Weakened** (-2 to all d20, per the core, stacking). **Three stacks = Out Cold:**
  Helpless, your cups frozen where they stand.
- **The off cup.** A cup that has gone off is the same save at a further **-2**, and it
  counts as **2 cups** for the tally and the pile-up penalty. A failed save against an
  off cup still costs only one stack; the sting is the steeper roll and the double
  count.
- **An approach each round.** A contestant takes one of four:
  - **Fortitude:** drink a cup. Gain the cup, make the Body save above.
  - **Guile:** empty a cup unseen (DEX **Check** vs 12, a **Class Check** for a Thief).
    Success banks the cup with no drink. **Fail by any margin = caught spilling, -3
    cups.**
  - **Charm:** a CHA **Check** vs 12 to make a rival drink an extra or an off cup.
  - **Wits:** an INT **Check** vs 12 to read which cups are off, or to **pace** (your
    next drink ignores the pile-up penalty).
- **Weakened bites the whole table.** The -2 per stack hits the approach Checks and the
  Body saves alike, so the drunker you are the clumsier your guile and the weaker your
  gut.
- **Win:** most cups at the last bell, **among contestants still conscious.** Stock runs
  six rounds.

Saves are `1d20 + half Level + Ability Mod` vs 12; at Level 2 half-level is +1, and the
class save boon (Fighter and Ranger +2 Body) rides on top. Checks are `1d20 + Ability
Mod`; a Class Check adds Level. The optional Natural 20/1 rule is **off** (1e reading).

## The party (Level 2, a deliberate CON spread)

Contest-relevant lines only. Body is the full drinking save before the pile-up penalty.

- **Dagny, Fighter.** CON 16 (+2), **Body +5**. Wits -1, Guile +0, Charm +0. The gut.
- **Grukk, Barbarian.** CON 18 (+3), **Body +4**. Wits -1, Guile +0, Charm -1. Highest raw CON, no Body boon.
- **Wen, Ranger.** CON 13 (+1), **Body +4**. Wits +0, Guile +1, Charm +0.
- **Aldric, Cleric.** CON 11 (0), **Body +1**. Wits +1, Guile +0, Charm +1.
- **Pib, Thief.** CON 9 (0), **Body +1**. Wits +1, **Guile +4** (Class Check), Charm +1. Weak gut, sly hands.
- **Ves, Magic-User.** CON 8 (-1), **Body 0**. **Wits +2**, Guile +0, Charm +0. Cannot drink, can scheme.

## The house (GM-run, to drive the table)

- **Old Bessa**, a dwarf drover. **Body +6.** Pure fortitude, sets a brutal pace. The benchmark to beat.
- **Finch**, a peddler. **Guile +4.** Pours out, needles others toward spills.
- **Mad Gurt**, a hog farmer. **Body +2.** Drinks everything, off cups and all. Comic fuse.
- **Goodwife Harl**, tapster and judge. Watches the hands for spills.

## Outcome

At the bell, by cups, conscious contestants only:

- **Old Bessa (house) 8.** The benchmark stands.
- **Dagny, Fighter, 6** — top of the party, still on her feet at one stack.
- **Aldric, Cleric, 5** (two stacks).
- **Ves, Magic-User, 3** (two stacks).
- **Pib, Thief, 1** — wrecked by the final spill.
- **Out Cold:** Grukk 6, Gurt 6, Wen 5, all frozen, none eligible to win.

**No PC beat the house.** Dagny won the party's side of the table on fortitude alone;
the two highest-cup totals on the board, Grukk's and Gurt's 6, belonged to drinkers
passed out under it. The schemers kept their feet but not their lead.

## Findings

1. **The Body-save ladder works and resolves on its own.** The cumulative -1 per cup
   means no gut holds forever: Dagny at Body +5 still failed in Round 4 and would have
   dropped by Round 8. CON sets how long you last, not whether you last, which is the
   point. The contest never stalled and needed no round cap to end, the cap just set
   the bell.

2. **CON matters exactly as intended, maybe too cleanly for off cups.** The spread of
   Body +5 down to 0 produced a clean order of endurance. But the off cup's flat -2 is
   trivial to a high-CON drinker (Bessa passed two off cups without a stack) and brutal
   to a low one. The off cup differentiates guts even more than clean cups do. If that
   is the wanted texture, keep it; if off cups should threaten everyone, make the off
   cup a **fixed-target** save (say 15, no CON) so the bad ale is bad ale for all.

3. **The -3 spill is swingy against single-cup gains.** Pib's whole game was guile, and
   one failed pour-out at the bell erased three rounds of banking. That is dramatic and
   it did drive the story, but -3 against a +1-per-cup economy means one slip is three
   turns gone. Consider **-2**, or making the spill cost scale with how badly the Check
   failed. Flag either way: the number wants a deliberate choice, not a default.

4. **Weakened coupling is the best part.** Because the -2 stacks hit the approach Checks
   too, drinking makes guile and charm sloppier, which is what tipped Pib's safe +4
   pour-out into a -3 disaster once he had a stack. The drunker you get the worse every
   path becomes, so there is no free lane. This coupling is worth keeping verbatim.

5. **The clever low-CON path survives but cannot win on cups.** Ves weaponized the
   telegraph (Wits to spot off cups, Charm to hand them to Bessa) and Wits-pacing kept
   her conscious at CON 8, which is a real achievement. But scheming never **scores**:
   Charm and Wits gain no cups, and Guile gains one at the risk of three. The schemers
   ended last on the tally. If a non-drinker should be able to **win**, not just
   survive, the sub-game needs a cup reward: e.g. a successful **Charm** that makes a
   rival drink also lets you pour one of yours out for a cup, or a clean **Guile**
   pour-out banks **2** against the off-cup's double. Without that, this is a fortitude
   contest with flavor, and CON simply wins.

6. **"Conscious at the bell" is an unstated but load-bearing ruling.** The two highest
   cup totals belonged to the unconscious. The contest only makes sense if passing out
   disqualifies you from winning, which the premise never says. State it: you must be
   on your stool at the last bell to claim the pot.

7. **The telegraph did its job.** The one-in-three off cup, read a beat early by smell
   and sheen, turned into a resource the table fought over rather than a random gotcha.
   Wits spotting and Charm foisting made the off cups the most interactive part of the
   night. That is the telegraph-as-counterplay idea working outside combat.

## Proposed next pass

If run again: off cups as a flat DC 15 save (finding 2), spill at -2 (finding 3), a
cup reward on a clean Charm or a doubled Guile bank (finding 5), and the
conscious-at-the-bell rule written into the premise (finding 6). Then test whether a
CON 8 schemer can actually take the pot off Old Bessa.
