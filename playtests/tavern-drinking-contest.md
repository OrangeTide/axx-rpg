# AX&X Solo Playtest: The Clear-Out Cup

A rules test for a non-combat contest, built to lean on **Constitution** and the
save math rather than attacks. The Gilded Sow is clearing old stock with a drinking
contest: most empty cups in front of you at the last bell wins. A spill caught by
the house costs **3 cups**. Roughly **one cup in three has gone off**, telegraphed by
a sour reek and an oily sheen. The question this run asks: does a Body-save ladder
make CON matter, resolve on its own, and still leave room for a clever low-CON
character to compete?

## The rulings under test

Settled before the run, flagged here so the log can be read against them.

- **The drink.** Drinking a cup is a **Body save** vs 12, **minus 1 per cup already in
  front of you** (the drink piling up). Pass: nothing but merrier. Fail: one stack of
  **Weakened** (-2 to all d20, per the core, stacking). **Three stacks = Out Cold:**
  Helpless, your cups frozen where they stand.
- **The off cup.** A cup that has gone off is the same save at a further **-2**, and it
  counts as **2 cups** for the tally and the pile-up penalty. A failed save against an
  off cup still costs only one stack; the sting is the steeper roll and the double
  count.
- **An approach each round.** A contestant takes one of four:
  - **Fortitude:** drink a cup. Gain the cup, make the Body save above.
  - **Guile:** empty a cup unseen (DEX **Check** vs 12, a **Class Check** for a Thief).
    Success banks the cup with no drink. **Fail by any margin = caught spilling, -3
    cups.**
  - **Charm:** a CHA **Check** vs 12 to make a rival drink an extra or an off cup.
  - **Wits:** an INT **Check** vs 12 to read which cups are off, or to **pace** (your
    next drink ignores the pile-up penalty).
- **Weakened bites the whole table.** The -2 per stack hits the approach Checks and the
  Body saves alike, so the drunker you are the clumsier your guile and the weaker your
  gut.
- **Win:** most cups at the last bell, **among contestants still conscious.** Stock runs
  six rounds.

Saves are `1d20 + half Level + Ability Mod` vs 12; at Level 2 half-level is +1, and the
class save boon (Fighter and Ranger +2 Body) rides on top. Checks are `1d20 + Ability
Mod`; a Class Check adds Level. The optional Natural 20/1 rule is **off** (1e reading).

## The party (Level 2, a deliberate CON spread)

Contest-relevant lines only. Body is the full drinking save before the pile-up penalty.

- **Dagny, Fighter.** CON 16 (+2), **Body +5**. Wits -1, Guile +0, Charm +0. The gut.
- **Grukk, Barbarian.** CON 18 (+3), **Body +4**. Wits -1, Guile +0, Charm -1. Highest raw CON, no Body boon.
- **Wen, Ranger.** CON 13 (+1), **Body +4**. Wits +0, Guile +1, Charm +0.
- **Aldric, Cleric.** CON 11 (0), **Body +1**. Wits +1, Guile +0, Charm +1.
- **Pib, Thief.** CON 9 (0), **Body +1**. Wits +1, **Guile +4** (Class Check), Charm +1. Weak gut, sly hands.
- **Ves, Magic-User.** CON 8 (-1), **Body 0**. **Wits +2**, Guile +0, Charm +0. Cannot drink, can scheme.

## The house (GM-run, to drive the table)

- **Old Bessa**, a dwarf drover. **Body +6.** Pure fortitude, sets a brutal pace. The benchmark to beat.
- **Finch**, a peddler. **Guile +4.** Pours out, needles others toward spills.
- **Mad Gurt**, a hog farmer. **Body +2.** Drinks everything, off cups and all. Comic fuse.
- **Goodwife Harl**, tapster and judge. Watches the hands for spills.

## Outcome

At the bell, by cups, conscious contestants only:

- **Old Bessa (house) 8.** The benchmark stands.
- **Dagny, Fighter, 6** — top of the party, still on her feet at one stack.
- **Aldric, Cleric, 5** (two stacks).
- **Ves, Magic-User, 3** (two stacks).
- **Pib, Thief, 1** — wrecked by the final spill.
- **Out Cold:** Grukk 6, Gurt 6, Wen 5, all frozen, none eligible to win.

**No PC beat the house.** Dagny won the party's side of the table on fortitude alone;
the two highest-cup totals on the board, Grukk's and Gurt's 6, belonged to drinkers
passed out under it. The schemers kept their feet but not their lead.

## Findings

1. **The Body-save ladder works and resolves on its own.** The cumulative -1 per cup
   means no gut holds forever: Dagny at Body +5 still failed in Round 4 and would have
   dropped by Round 8. CON sets how long you last, not whether you last, which is the
   point. The contest never stalled and needed no round cap to end, the cap just set
   the bell.

2. **CON matters exactly as intended, maybe too cleanly for off cups.** The spread of
   Body +5 down to 0 produced a clean order of endurance. But the off cup's flat -2 is
   trivial to a high-CON drinker (Bessa passed two off cups without a stack) and brutal
   to a low one. The off cup differentiates guts even more than clean cups do. If that
   is the wanted texture, keep it; if off cups should threaten everyone, make the off
   cup a **fixed-target** save (say 15, no CON) so the bad ale is bad ale for all.

3. **The -3 spill is swingy against single-cup gains.** Pib's whole game was guile, and
   one failed pour-out at the bell erased three rounds of banking. That is dramatic and
   it did drive the story, but -3 against a +1-per-cup economy means one slip is three
   turns gone. Consider **-2**, or making the spill cost scale with how badly the Check
   failed. Flag either way: the number wants a deliberate choice, not a default.

4. **Weakened coupling is the best part.** Because the -2 stacks hit the approach Checks
   too, drinking makes guile and charm sloppier, which is what tipped Pib's safe +4
   pour-out into a -3 disaster once he had a stack. The drunker you get the worse every
   path becomes, so there is no free lane. This coupling is worth keeping verbatim.

5. **The clever low-CON path survives but cannot win on cups.** Ves weaponized the
   telegraph (Wits to spot off cups, Charm to hand them to Bessa) and Wits-pacing kept
   her conscious at CON 8, which is a real achievement. But scheming never **scores**:
   Charm and Wits gain no cups, and Guile gains one at the risk of three. The schemers
   ended last on the tally. If a non-drinker should be able to **win**, not just
   survive, the sub-game needs a cup reward: e.g. a successful **Charm** that makes a
   rival drink also lets you pour one of yours out for a cup, or a clean **Guile**
   pour-out banks **2** against the off-cup's double. Without that, this is a fortitude
   contest with flavor, and CON simply wins.

6. **"Conscious at the bell" is an unstated but load-bearing ruling.** The two highest
   cup totals belonged to the unconscious. The contest only makes sense if passing out
   disqualifies you from winning, which the premise never says. State it: you must be
   on your stool at the last bell to claim the pot.

7. **The telegraph did its job.** The one-in-three off cup, read a beat early by smell
   and sheen, turned into a resource the table fought over rather than a random gotcha.
   Wits spotting and Charm foisting made the off cups the most interactive part of the
   night. That is the telegraph-as-counterplay idea working outside combat.

## Proposed next pass

If run again: off cups as a flat DC 15 save (finding 2), spill at -2 (finding 3), a
cup reward on a clean Charm or a doubled Guile bank (finding 5), and the
conscious-at-the-bell rule written into the premise (finding 6). Then test whether a
CON 8 schemer can actually take the pot off Old Bessa.

# Second Pass: The CON-Drain Rules

The rules the tavern supplement now carries, tested against the same party. The
pile-up penalty is gone; drinking instead **drains CON**, which lowers the very mod
the drinking save rolls on, restored by a night's rest. Spill is -2, off cups are a
flat DC 15 (CON no help), smelling a cup is a real **Wits (INT) Check** that can fail,
and the Cleric brings **Purify Food and Drink**. Played loose: beats narrated, dice
only on the deliberate acts.

## The rulings as run

- **Drink:** -1 CON (off -2). **Body save at current CON mod** vs 12. Fail = **lurch**
  (Weakened -2 on your next action only). **Pass out at CON 3.**
- **Smell** (Wits INT Check vs 12, 15 if doctored) to tell clean from turned, costs the turn.
- **Guile** pour-out banks a cup; caught = -2. **Charm** presses a cup on a rival. **Purify** cleans a turned cup to ordinary ale.
- Body save = `d20 + 1 (half-level) + current CON mod + class Body boon` (Fighter and Ranger +2).

## The night

Starting CON: Dagny 16, Grukk 18, Wen 13, Aldric 11, Pib 9, Ves 8; Old Bessa the dwarf benchmark at an effective Body +5.

**Pours 1-2.** Grukk scorns a sniff and drinks a **turned** cup blind, flat 15 `[9]`,
fail: even an 18-CON gut lurches on a mouthful of sour (CON 18 to 16). Dagny `[11]`,
Wen (smelled clean first, `[13]`) and Aldric drink clean and pass. **Pib opens
cheating**, Guile `[13]`+4 = 17, banks a cup with no drink. **Ves** reads the table,
Wits `[14]`+2, and spends pour 2 **Charming a turned cup onto Old Bessa** `[15]`. The
dwarf, a sport, drinks it, flat 15 `[14]`, **fail, lurches**: the flat DC humbles even
Bessa. But note what the Charm did: it handed Bessa a cup, which **raised** his tally.
**Pib is caught** on his second pour-out `[7]`+4 = 11, -2, back to nothing. **Aldric
spends a Purify slot** to cleanse a turned cup and drink it safe.

Standings after 2: Dagny, Aldric, Wen, Grukk, Bessa all 2; Ves and Pib 0. The two
schemers are last.

**Pours 3-5.** The drinkers grind. Nearly every Body save passes, and the few that
fail (Grukk twice, Wen once, Aldric once) cost only a **lurch**, never the cup. Dagny
rolls down from CON 16 toward 11 but keeps scoring one a round. Aldric burns his
second Purify to mine another turned cup. Pib plays safe Guile `[17]`, `[14]` and
climbs slowly; Ves drinks what little she dares, failing at CON 8 `[9]` but losing
only a lurch, and creeps to 2. Old Bessa matches the drinkers cup for cup.

Standings after 5: Dagny, Aldric, Wen, Bessa, Grukk all 5; Pib 3; Ves 2.

**Pour 6, the bell.** Everyone who drinks scores their sixth. Dagny `[12]`, Grukk
(fails, lurches, keeps the cup), Wen `[14]`, Aldric `[15]`, Bessa `[11]` all reach 6.
**Pib is caught a second time** `[6]`+4 = 10, -2, and finishes at 1. Ves lands her
clean cup, 3. **Not one contestant passed out:** the highest-CON drinkers ended around
CON 10-11, nowhere near the floor of 3.

## Outcome

**A five-way tie at 6 cups:** Dagny, Grukk, Wen, Aldric, and Old Bessa, every one of
them still upright. Pib 1, Ves 2. The tie forced a ruling the rules do not have; I
settled it on **steadiest hand, the highest CON still standing**, which gave it to
**Grukk** (CON 11) over Bessa (CON 9). So the party beat the house, barely, and only
on a tiebreak invented at the table.

## Findings

1. **The flat-DC off cup is the fix it promised.** A turned cup now humbles anyone, an
   18-CON barbarian and an iron dwarf both lurched on one. Off cups are a shared
   hazard, not a frailty tax. Keep it.

2. **Smell-as-a-Check feels right.** Wen and Aldric both failed to read their cups and
   drank blind; Ves, with the wits for it, turned the telegraph into a weapon. Fallible
   and tempo-costed, exactly as wanted.

3. **Purify stayed balanced and useful.** Aldric spent both slots to drink turned cups
   safely, a real edge against the 1-in-3, but it cost him two turns and never touched
   the drunkenness race. It made the Cleric a support piece, not a winner. Good.

4. **The core flaw: a failed drink costs only a lurch, so a cup is nearly free.** This
   is the big result. Because failing the Body save does not cost the cup or the turn,
   anyone who drinks every round scores every round, and they **all tie at 6**. CON
   drain and the lurch add flavor but never gate the score. The contest needs the
   failure to bite the tally: a failed save should mean **the cup does not count** (you
   wear it) or **you lose your next pour** (retching), not just a -2 on one later roll.

5. **Pass-out at CON 3 never fired.** Six pours drain a high gut only to CON 10-11, so
   the dramatic topple that ended the first run never happened here and the night
   ended in a tie instead. The first pass's **three-lurch ladder to Out Cold** actually
   produced pass-outs and a clean winner. The cleanest design is probably **both**: CON
   drain for the diegetic difficulty, plus **three lurches = Out Cold** so failing
   accumulates toward a real fall (which also answers finding 4).

6. **A tiebreaker is mandatory, and it exposes the contest as a CON check.** Five
   drinkers tied, and the only sensible tiebreak, highest CON standing, just crowns the
   toughest gut. Under the pure-drain rules the Clear-Out Cup reduces to "who has the
   most Constitution," with the other three roads as decoration.

7. **The schemers lost again, worse.** Pib's Guile was efficient between catches, but
   two -2 spills across the night sank him to last. And **Charm is actively
   counterproductive:** making a rival drink *raises their cup count*, so Ves's clever
   foisting fed Bessa's lead while only tiring his gut. For the clever roads to matter,
   **a caught spill must sting less or a Charmed rival must lose a cup or a turn**, not
   gain one.

## Verdict

The supplement's rules play clean and loose at the table, and off cups, smelling, and
Purify all land well. But as a **contest** the pure-CON-drain version is
under-differentiated: drinking is a free cup, nobody falls, and everyone ties. The
recommended patch, carried back into the supplement, is small: **a failed Body save
means the cup does not count**, and **three lurches drops you Out Cold**. That restores
the first run's real toppling and its clear finish while keeping the CON-drain spine.
Charm and Guile still need a reason to exist beside straight drinking; that is the next
dial to turn.

**Applied:** the failed-cup and three-lurch-to-Out-Cold rules are now in the tavern
supplement's Clear-Out Cup. The Charm and Guile scoring question is left open.

# Third Pass: Teeth for the Schemers

Testing the supplement's new **Cheat and Sabotage** options, which read the pot as
relative, so tearing a rival down wins as well as drinking. The question: can a low-CON
schemer now take the pot off Old Bessa? Pib the Thief plays the pure cheat (ditch cups
to score, spill rivals'), Ves the Magic-User plays the saboteur (Wits to read, Charm to
foist turned cups and drive rivals under). The rest drink.

## The rulings as run

- **Ditch a cup** (Guile, DEX or Thief Class Check vs 12): scores an empty cup, no drink; caught = -2 cups.
- **Spill a rival's** cup (Guile vs 12): the house docks them 2.
- **Foist a turned cup** (Charm vs 12): the rival drinks it; a failed flat-15 sour save scores them nothing and lands a lurch. Three lurches, Out Cold.
- Pib's ditch/spill is a Class Check, `d20 + 2 (Level) + 2 (DEX) = +4`; Ves's Charm is `d20 + 0`.

## The night

**Pours 1-2.** Pib opens ditching, `[12]`+4 and `[9]`+4, two clean cups and not a drop
drunk. Grukk drinks blind into a **turned** cup, flat 15 `[8]`: under the new rule he
scores **nothing** and lurches. Ves reads the table (`[14]`+2), then **foists a turned
cup on Grukk** `[13]`: he drinks, fails `[7]`, a second lurch. The drinkers, Dagny,
Wen, Aldric, Bessa, bank their cups. Aldric fails one himself, `[8]`, and wears it.

Standings after 2: Dagny, Wen, Bessa, Pib all 2; Aldric 1; Ves 0; Grukk 0 and two
lurches deep.

**Pour 3, the barbarian falls.** Ves foists a third turned cup on Grukk `[16]`; he
drinks, `[5]`, **third lurch, Out Cold.** The saboteur has removed the strongest gut at
the table without lifting a cup. But the night's cheating catches up: **Pib is caught**
tipping his out, `[6]`+4 = 10, and forfeits 2, back to nothing. Ves has scored zero,
all her turns spent on Grukk.

Standings after 3: Dagny, Wen, Bessa 3; Aldric 2; Pib 0, Ves 0; Grukk out.

**Pours 4-6.** With Grukk gone, the schemers turn to scoring, but the math is unkind.
Pib ditches clean (`[15]`, `[12]`, `[11]`) and climbs, Ves finally banks a few (`[13]`,
`[14]`), but Wen is **caught** ditching too (`[8]`+1), -2, and the steady drinkers pull
away at a reliable cup a round. At the last pour Ves spends her turn **foisting a turned
cup on Dagny** `[15]`, denying the leader her sixth (`[8]` sour, no cup, a lurch), but
it is a cup denied, not a cup gained. Old Bessa, never once sabotaged, drinks her sixth
clean.

## Outcome

**Old Bessa takes it at 6**, still upright. Dagny 5 (robbed of her last by Ves). Aldric
4, Pib 3, Wen 3, Ves 2. Grukk out cold. The house wins a third time, and both schemers
finished in the bottom three.

## Findings

1. **The sabotage works, and it is the best new toy.** Ves put the 18-CON barbarian
   under the bench with three foisted cups and never drank a drop, and her last foist
   robbed Dagny of the winning cup. Foisting a turned cup is now a real play because the
   patched rules mean the victim scores nothing on the failed sour save. Removing and
   denying rivals is fun and it swings the board.

2. **But sabotage does not score, so a lone saboteur sinks.** Every turn Ves spent
   foisting was a turn she banked no cup, and she finished last but one. Disruption is a
   spoiler's game, not a winner's: you can drag the field down, but you cannot climb it
   at the same time.

3. **The cheat is a mathematical trap.** This is the key result. Ditching at a 12+
   check with a -2 catch is **expected-value negative even for the Thief**: Pib's +4
   succeeds about 65% of the time, so each ditch averages `0.65(+1) + 0.35(-2) = -0.05`
   cups. Meanwhile a drinker passes more often and a failed drink now costs only the cup
   (zero), never going negative. So **drinking strictly beats cheating**, and the two
   catches (Pib, Wen) were not bad luck, they were the expected tax. Guile cannot be a
   scoring lane while the catch is -2 against a 1-in-3 failure.

4. **One or two schemers cannot suppress a whole field.** Ves removed Grukk and denied
   Dagny, real damage, but Old Bessa was never touched and strolled to 6. With five
   drinkers and two schemers, there are not enough sabotage actions to hold everyone
   down, so the unharried drinker wins. Schemers are kingmakers and spoilers here, not
   favorites.

## Verdict and the next dial

The new tools are good: the table now has lateral play, a barbarian got foisted under
the bench, a leader was robbed at the bell, and it read as a bawdy, dirty contest
rather than a CON check. But the original goal, that a clever low-CON character can
**win**, is still unmet, for one concrete reason: **ditching loses cups on average.**
The fix is to make the cheat pay in a room this rowdy and crowded:

- **Ditching is an easy act** (9+, not 12+) when the room is packed and loud, and the
  **catch is -1**, not -2. Then the Thief's ditch averages about `+0.7` a round, matched
  to drinking, and a cheat who is never sabotaged can run with the drinkers.
- Leave sabotage as it is. It should stay a spoiler's tool that trades your turn for a
  rival's, not a self-scoring one, or disruption becomes dominant.

With ditching made to pay, a fourth pass could finally answer the question with a yes:
a Thief who cheats every cup while an ally foists the leaders under the bench.
