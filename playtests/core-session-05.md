# AX&X Solo Playtest, Session 05: The Boss Gamble

A single-encounter what-if. It takes **Session 04's party** at the point where they
reached the Drowned Warden and chose to retreat, and instead has them **commit to
the fight**, to see what happens when a party presses its luck against a wall rather
than making the safe play. Current rules, softened Brush table, neutral GM.

The party's one real answer to the boss is fire: the Warden is vulnerable to it, and
between them they carry **ten oil flasks** (Orsible 4, Pip 4, Hurgo 2), each doubled
against the vulnerability. The plan is to commit to that: Branna opens with Bless
(+1 to all party attacks and saves), Hurgo melees to hold its attention, and
everyone hurls oil.

## State at the Bell

- Hurgo (Fighter): 8 HP, AC 15, sword 1d6+1, 2 oil.
- Branna (Cleric): 6 HP, AC 14, 2 slots (Bless, Cure Light Wounds). Turn already spent and failed.
- Pip (Thief): 5 HP, AC 11, 4 oil, sling.
- Orsible (Magic-User): 4 HP, AC 10, 4 oil, no spells.

Warden: HD 5, HP 40, AC 15, two slams +5 (1d8), **vulnerable to fire (oil doubles)**,
undead, saves 15, the telegraphed Undertow.

## A ruling, flagged

Two of the oil hits below were natural 20s, which the core makes double damage
(line 68), landing on a target that already takes fire as double. The core does not
say whether those two doublings stack. For this run I ruled that **a damage result
is doubled at most once; multiple doubling sources do not stack** (times two, not
times four). It is the terse, non-explosive reading, but the core should say so
outright. Under the stacking reading the Warden would have died a round sooner.

## The Fight

### Round 1 (Warden 40, init party `[4]` vs warden `[1]`, party first)

- Branna casts **Bless**. Hurgo's sword `[14]`+2 = 16 hits for 3. Orsible's oil `[5]` misses. **Pip's oil `[19]`+1 hits**, doubled fire 5 x 2 = 10. Warden 40 -> 27.
- Warden slams Hurgo (AC 15): `[5]` miss, `[17]`+5 = 22 hit for 6. Hurgo 8 -> 2.

### Round 2 (Warden 27, init party `[6]` vs warden `[4]`, party first)

- Branna spends her last slot on Cure Light Wounds for Hurgo, `[2]`, to 4.
- **Hurgo's oil is a natural `[20]`**, doubled to 12; **Orsible's oil is also a natural `[20]`**, doubled to 8. Warden 27 -> 7. Pip's oil `[8]` misses.
- Warden slams Hurgo: `[17]`+5 and `[23]` both hit, 6 and 1, total 7. Hurgo 4 -> 0.
- **Hurgo Brush `[1]` = Close Call.** The slam that felled him is negated; he stays up at 4.

### Round 3 (Warden 7, init party `[3]` vs warden `[1]`, party first)

- Orsible's oil `[15]`+1 = 16 hits for doubled 4 (Warden 7 -> 3). **Pip's oil is a natural `[20]`**, doubled to 12. **Warden destroyed.** It never got to rear, so the Undertow never came.

## Outcome

**Victory in 3 rounds, no deaths.** Hurgo dropped once and came back on a Close
Call; everyone else was untouched. The party claims the reliquary (Art, 300 GP) on
top of the 600 GP orc cache.

- Warden: 125 XP. Reliquary: 300 GP. With the rest of the Session 04 delve, this is a full clear: ghoul 50, depth 100, orcs 75, skeletons 75, Warden 125, plus 900 gold.

## Notes

1. **The boss is not an unconditional wall. Fire is the answer, and it works.** The
   Warden has 40 HP and two +5 slams, which is hopeless to grind down with swords at
   single-digit damage, as Sessions 03 and 04 found. But it is vulnerable to fire,
   and a party that commits ten oil flasks against that vulnerability, with Bless up
   to help them land, burns it down fast. Every point of damage that mattered this
   fight was fire. The telegraphed trick (Undertow) never even fired because the
   fight was too short.

2. **Session 04's "smart retreat" may have left a winnable fight on the table.**
   That party carried the same ten oil flasks and withdrew without throwing one. The
   lesson is not that retreat was wrong, it was a reasonable read, but that the
   party had the tool to win and did not recognize it. The vulnerability line in a
   stat block is the invitation; a party that reads it and commits can beat a foe
   that looks like a wall. This is exactly the "press your luck" risk the game is
   built on: the gamble was live because the party was correctly equipped.

3. **The win was dice-aided, and the gamble was real, not a sure thing.** Three oil
   flasks came up natural 20 across three rounds, which is hot. Strip the crits and
   the math is a genuine race: oil at roughly a third to hit (with Bless), doubled
   to about 7, across three throwers is near 7 or 8 a round against 40 HP, five-plus
   rounds, while the Warden drops a low-HP character every round or two and the
   Undertow waits. That fight could easily have gone the other way, with a drowned
   mage and a dragged cleric. The point is that committing to fire makes it a
   coin-flip the party can win, where melee-only makes it a loss.

4. **The softened Brush table turned the one drop into a save.** Hurgo's Close Call
   on a `[1]` negated the slam that would have put him on the old table's path to
   Slain. The gentler table is what lets a party gamble at all: a drop is a scare,
   not an automatic funeral, so pressing your luck is a real decision rather than
   russian roulette.

5. **Crit-times-vulnerability needs a line in the rules.** The result hinged on a
   ruling the core does not make. Whether two doublings stack to times four or cap
   at times two is the difference between the Warden dying in two rounds or three,
   and against a squishier target it is the difference between a hit and a corpse.
   One sentence in the Natural 20 rule would settle it.
