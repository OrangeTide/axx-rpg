# AX&X Playtest: The Highcairn Caravan

A solo, GM-and-players-both run exercising the **core rules** (`src/axx.typ`)
together with the **Travel**, **Warbands**, and **Retinue** supplements. The job:
escort a merchant caravan from the mountain town of Highcairn, down a dangerous
forest road, across a raised swamp causeway, to the riverside trade town of
Rivermouth. Goals for the run: stress overland travel with carts, a non-combatant
train, and a deadline; exercise mass combat; and test the new delayed **Brush
with Death** rule (the roll now falls at the start of your next turn, with a
one-round window for allies to heal or steady you). Every die was a real shell
roll, quoted inline as `[dice] = total`.

## The Contract

Six merchants hire the party to see six laden carts and their people safe to
Rivermouth by a fixed date. The short way runs through a Tier III forest and then
a swamp crossed only by a raised causeway, single-file, carts nose to tail.

The clock is a **Journey Count of 6, "the Flood"**: storm season has begun, and
the causeway floods when the Count fills. It gains a mark for each Mishap and each
night camped in the open. When it fills, the water rises over the causeway and
the contract is as good as lost unless they are already across.

Region Tiers: the pass out of Highcairn is **Tier I-II**, the forest road is
**Tier III** (the deadly stretch), the swamp is **Tier II**.

## The Party

Five characters, abilities rolled 3d6 in order, then class and kindred chosen to
suit. Modifier table: 3 = -3, 4-5 = -2, 6-8 = -1, 9-12 = 0, 13-15 = +1, 16-17 =
+2, 18 = +3. Level-1 saves carry no half-level, so a save is the ability modifier
plus class and kindred boons.

## The Caravan and the Militia

Six carts, each a small horse team, foot distance, road or trail only. Six
merchants, no fighters. Of the thirty-strong entourage, **ten are armed teamsters**
run as a Warband per the supplement: 10 members = 2 files x HD 1 = **Warband HD 2**
(HP 10, AC 12, attack 1d20 +2 for 1d6+2, save +2), under the caravan master as
Commander. The other twenty are drivers, cooks, and servants: liabilities, not a
warband, and the reason the party cannot simply run.

## The Journey

Wren guides every leg (overland marches on STR, so 1d20 +1). The party soon
learns to post a second traveler to **Aid** the guide for +2.

### Leg 1 — the pass road out of Highcairn (Tier I-II)

Mountain grade -2, but a kept pass road, so a net +2. Travel `[19]` +1 +2 = 22, an
easy success. Encounter die `[2]`, nothing. They reach the forest edge and camp.
**Count 1.**

### Leg 2 — into the forest, pushing for time (Tier III)

With the deadline on their minds they hold **Steady** pace rather than Cautious.
Travel `[4]` +1 +4 (trail), with one teamster's Aid +2 = 11, one short of 12.
**Failure.** They reach the next clearing but lose a day and roll Mishaps `[4]` =
**Not Alone**. A wandering foe, read off the forest at Tier III: an **orc patrol**,
six orcs (HD 1, AC 13, attack +1, 1d6, ~4 HP), two carrying shortbows. **Count 3**
(the Mishap, and the night).

**The ambush.** Surprise: party `[1]`, orcs `[3]`. The party rolled a 1, so even
Wren is caught (his trait saves him only *when the roll is not a 1*). The orcs
break cover at `[1 4]`x10 = 50 ft. The two bowmen loose at the obvious mark, Nix
at the rear: `[12]` +1 = 13 and `[10]` +1 = 11, both over AC 10, for `[3]` + `[3]`
= 6. **Nix, 4 HP, drops to 0.** Damage past 0 is ignored, so the arrows cannot do
worse. The other four orcs charge in from 50 ft, reaching the line. The party,
surprised, does nothing this round.

Under the old rule Nix would have rolled Brush with Death on the spot, and a bad
roll ends her before the party moves. Under the new rule she is down, Helpless,
and **rolls at the start of her next turn** — the party has a round.

**Round 1.** Initiative: party `[1]`, orcs `[1]`, a tie, so all act at once,
resolved before anyone drops. The players reason it out: Nix cannot be hurt
further at 0, and Dura holds **only one spell slot**, too precious to spend on a
mage who might recover on her own. So **Pib spends his action to steady Nix: -2
to her roll** (the new assist). The melee:

- Gorm `[17]` +1 = 18 vs AC 13, hit, `[5]` +1 = 6, an orc down.
- Wren `[15]` +1 = 16, hit, `[5]`, an orc down.
- Dura maces a charger, `[20]` +1 (a natural 20, read as an ordinary hit, 1e mode), `[2]`, orc to 2 HP.

The four chargers swing (charge +2): `[13]` +3 = 16 hits Gorm for `[4]` (to 5);
`[10]` +3 = 13 misses Dura; `[8]` +3 = 11 hits Wren for `[2]` (to 5); `[12]` +3 =
15 hits the kneeling Pib for `[5]` (to 1). Then **Nix's turn comes and she rolls
Brush with Death at -2**: `[2]` - 2 = 0, floored to the top of the table =
**Close Call**. The felling arrows are negated; she comes to at 4 HP, having spent
the turn getting up. Orc morale on first loss `[6]`: they hold.

**Round 2.** Orcs win initiative `[3]` over `[1]` and shoot, but Nix is on her
feet with Sleep still prepared. She casts: **Sleep** `[1]` + `[2]` = 3 HD, and
three of the four surviving orcs fold where they stand, no save. The last orc,
alone, is run down (Wren `[20]`). Afterward Dura spends her **one** Cure Light
Wounds on Pib, `[5]`, from 1 to 6.

Tally: all six orcs dead or taken, no one slain. Three of five PCs ended below
half, and the Cleric is now out of magic for the day. Nix lived only because the
party spent an action to pull her back.

### Legs 3-4 — through the forest, the careful way (Tier III)

Chastened, they drop to **Cautious** pace (+2, half distance) and keep a dedicated
Aider on the guide. Leg 3 travel `[4]` +1 +4 +2 +2 = 13, success. Leg 4 `[9]` +1 +4
+2 +2 = 18, success. Encounter dice `[5]` and `[6]`, quiet. But the careful pace
means more nights afield: **Count 4, then 5.** They clear the forest a step behind
schedule.

### Leg 5 — the swamp causeway, and the dead rise (Tier II)

Raised causeway, a trail through the mire, net -2. Travel `[13]` +1 -2 +2 = 14,
success, and they advance. But the daily encounter die comes up `[1]` — an
encounter, and on the narrow causeway there is nowhere to deploy. The Step `[5]`:
a step direr than Tier II. Read off the swamp as **undead in the mire**: fifteen
bog-skeletons heave up out of the water across the causeway ahead. As a Warband:
15 = 3 files x HD 1 = **Warband HD 3** (HP 15, AC 13, attack +3 for 1d6+3, Mindless,
**resistant to edged and piercing**).

Distance `[1 2]`x10 = 30 ft. Surprise `[3]`/`[3]`, neither side caught (Wren read
the bubbles). Initiative round 1: party `[4]`, host `[1]`, party first.

**Dura raises her holy symbol and Turns.** 1d20 `[10]` +1 = 11, against 10 + HD 1
= 11. Success. Turning routs **2d6 = `[5]` + `[6]` = 11 HD** of skeletons. Eleven
of the fifteen flee the symbol into the water; the four left are below a file and
**scatter on their own**. The clash is effectively over before a blade falls. The
militia and Dura's **blunt** mace (skeletons do not resist blunt) put down the
handful of stragglers over the next two rounds; the teamster warband loses a file
to a lucky skeleton swing (`[20]` read as an ordinary hit, one file gone), morale
`[4]`, they hold. No PC is touched: by the rules a host cannot bring enough blades
to splatter a lone fighter, and this one never got the chance.

Camp on the causeway that night fills the clock. **Count 6: the Flood.**

### Leg 6 — across the rising water to Rivermouth

The storm breaks upstream and the causeway begins to go under. Travel `[10]` +1 -2
+2, with Aid +2 = 13, success: they make the far bank, but it is a wet, roped
scramble. Cart check `[3]`: no cart bogs, all six come through. Teamsters and
servants cross on a line against the current, Dodge saves to keep their feet:
`[15]`, `[16]` hold, `[7]` and `[5]` fail and two laborers are swept off the
stones, and a teamster's `[20]` hauls them back by the rope. The caravan reaches
Rivermouth as the causeway disappears behind them, a day late off the Leg 2 loss,
soaked, down a cart-file of teamsters, but with all six carts and every merchant.
A late delivery, a docked fee, and a job done.

## Findings

**The delayed Brush with Death did exactly what it was meant to.** The orc ambush
is the clean test: the mage took two arrows in the surprise round and hit 0 before
the party could act. Under the instant rule the encounter is decided by that one
exchange, and probably by whether a single d6 reads 7+. Under the new rule the
party got a round, chose to spend Pib's action on the -2 assist rather than burn
the Cleric's only slot, and Nix came back. The decision was real and it cost
something (Pib took a hit kneeling in the open, then needed the Cure himself). The
rule turned a coin-flip death into a tactical beat. Keep it.

**The -2 assist is correctly priced.** A `[2]` became a Close Call, but note the
assist only *shifts* the die; it does not guarantee a good result. A natural 5 or
6 on the table still lands on Grievous or worse even after -2. It helps without
trivializing. The "only one aid counts" cap never came up here but would have
mattered if the whole party had piled on.

**"Damage past 0 is ignored" is load-bearing for the new rule.** Because the
downed mage could not be hit again, the rescue window was real and the orcs could
not simply finish her. If you ever add a coup-de-grace or a "strike the helpless"
option for monsters, it will directly undercut this rule. That is a design fork
worth deciding on purpose, not by accident.

**The one-slot Cleric is the quiet star of the run.** With WIS 10 Dura had a single
spell all day, and the rescue rule is what let her hold it: the free assist meant
she did not have to spend Cure Light Wounds on Nix, so she still had it for Pib
after the fight. Before this change, a one-slot Cleric is forced to burn the slot
the instant anyone drops, or watch them roll. The two rules together make low-WIS
Clerics playable rather than miserable.

**Turn Undead against a warband is enormous, maybe too much.** One roll routed 11
of 15 HD and ended the set-piece before it started. The Warband supplement says
Turn "routs Hit Dice of a skeleton legion," and 2d6 averages 7, so a Level 1
Cleric reliably erases a HD-3 undead host in a single action. Against the undead
warband that is supposed to be a crossing's climax, that is deflating. Worth a
look: cap the HD turned at the Cleric's Level, or have a warband make one Morale
save against the Turn rather than losing raw HD, so the holy symbol routs them as
a group instead of deleting them.

**Sleep is still a 1-HD-mob eraser, and the ambush knew it.** Three orcs folded to
one `[3]`-HD casting. The only reason the orc fight was a fight at all is that the
ambush dropped the caster before she could cast. That is less a rule to fix than a
reminder that targeting the mage first is the correct monster play, which the
surprise rules already reward.

**Travel with carts and a deadline worked without new dice.** The Steady-versus-
Cautious choice had teeth: pushing for time in the forest is exactly what caused
the Leg 2 Mishap and the one-day loss that made them late. Carts stayed sane on a
trail and never bogged on the causeway. The one soft spot is bookkeeping: I had to
rule that an open-camp night fills the Count, which is implied but not spelled out.
Consider stating plainly in Travel that a night in the open is a mark, so the
Flood-style clock ticks predictably.

**Nothing in the current rules broke.** The run touched creation, surprise,
initiative ties, charging, missile ambush, Sleep, Turn Undead, two warbands, a
terrain chokepoint, a Journey Count, and the new death timing, and every piece
resolved without a house rule except the open-camp mark above.
