# AX&X Solo Playtest, Session 01

A solo, GM-and-players-both run of the **core rules only** (`src/axx.typ`). Goal:
roll a party, open an adventure, reach a dungeon, and fight the first encounter
for 5 to 10 rounds or until it is decisive. Every die was a real shell roll; the
raw results are quoted inline as `[dice] = total`.

## The Adventure

Orsible's patron, a shrine of the Pale Lantern, pays 50 GP per map of a crypt
the spring floods have just cracked open under the river shires. The party takes
the work. Travel is a supplement and off-limits for this run, so the march is
narrated through in a sentence: they reach the broken slab over the stair and go
down.

**Location `[2]` = the Sunken Crypt**: knee-deep water, damp granite, the smell
of rot.

Stocking the entry chamber, 1d6: `[4]` = **monster**. Guard-treasure check `[5]`
= no hoard here, just the thing itself. Encounter roll on the core Threat table,
1d6: `[5]` = **Ghoul**.

> Ghoul. HD 2, HP 10, AC 14, Atk +2 (1d3). A hit: Body save or Paralyzed.
> Immune to Sleep, Charmed, Poisoned, Frightened. Saves at 10 + HD = 12.

The chamber: a ghoul crouched over a half-eaten corpse in the black water, and
the party sloshing in with a torch. Both sides rolled surprise, 1d6 each: party
`[1]`, ghoul `[1]`. By the rule each side on a 1-2 loses the first round, so the
first round is a mutual flinch where no one acts. The first round of real blows
is numbered Round 1 below.

Branna considered Turning the thing (1d20 + Level + WIS vs 12, so only 1d20+1
here, a long shot that would merely make it flee and cost the party the kill).
The band chose to put it down instead. Orsible's prepared Sleep is useless, the
ghoul is immune, so his Magic Missile (auto-hit) was the spell that mattered.

**Initiative, rolled once for the fight** (per the revised core rule): party
`[1]`, ghoul `[6]`. The ghoul acts first every round.

## The Fight

### Round 1 (ghoul 10 HP, acts first)

- **Ghoul** claws at Hurgo in the front: `[7]`+2 = 9 vs AC 14. **Miss.**
- **Hurgo** sword: `[14]`+3 = 17 vs 14. **Hit**, 1d6+1 = `[2]`+1 = **3**. Ghoul 10 -> 7.
- **Branna** mace: `[4]`+3 = 7 vs 14. **Miss.**
- **Pip** sling: `[5]`+3 = 8 vs 14. **Miss.**
- **Orsible** Magic Missile, auto-hit, 1d4+1 = `[1]`+1 = **2**. Ghoul 7 -> 5.

End of round: ghoul at 5, party untouched. Three of four attacks missed; against
AC 14 a +3 attacker needs an 11 on the die, and the dice ran cold.

### Round 2 (ghoul 5 HP, acts first)

- **Ghoul** claws at Hurgo again: `[9]`+2 = 11 vs AC 14. **Miss.**
- **Hurgo** sword: `[16]`+3 = 19 vs 14. **Hit**, 1d6+1 = `[4]`+1 = **5**. Ghoul 5 -> 0. **Down.**
- The rest of the round lands on a falling corpse as it drops: Branna `[18]`+3 hit, Pip `[14]`+3 hit, Orsible's oil flask `[15]`+0 hit for 3 fire. Overkill; the thing was already slain.

**Outcome: decisive party victory in 2 rounds. No character took damage.** The
ghoul whiffed both of its attacks (needing a 12, rolling 9 and 11), so its one
real threat, the paralysis-on-hit, never came into play.

## Aftermath and XP

No treasure in this chamber. XP earned:

- First visit to dungeon level 1: 100 XP x depth 1 = **100 XP**.
- Total **150 XP**, split four ways = about **37 XP each**, or pooled toward the
  2,000 needed for Level 2. (Gold is only XP once carried back to town, and none

The crypt continues: the next chamber stocked as treasure, the one after also
treasure, then a trap or trick, then empty. Plenty left to crawl.

## Playtest Notes

What the run surfaced, for review. Nothing here was changed; these are
observations from the table.

1. **A lone Threat-table monster is a speed bump for a four-PC party.** Four
   attacks a round against a single 10 HP body is roughly 15 expected damage,
   so even with three misses the ghoul fell in two rounds. The encounter never
   got dangerous because the monster could not act fast enough or hit hard
   enough before dying.

2. **The Threat table gives a count for some entries but not others.** Giant
   Rats (2d6), Goblin Band (1d6), Orc Patrol (1d4) come in numbers; Ghoul,
   Skeleton Guard, and Giant Spider read as singletons. A lone ghoul badly
   underperforms its slot next to "1d4 orc warriors." Worth deciding whether
   every Threat entry should carry a number appearing, or whether the countless
   ones are meant as the weaker rolls.

3. **Monster accuracy vs level-1 AC ran defender-favored.** The party bought
   AC 14 cheaply (light armor 13 + DEX or shield), and the ghoul attacks at only
   +HD = +2, so it needed a 12 and missed twice. This ties back to the open
   question about AC: "armor value + DEX" stacks full DEX on top of an armor
   value that already includes a base, so light armor + a +1 DEX already reaches
   AC 14 at level 1. A single 2-HD monster has a hard time landing a hit, which
   defused its save-or-lose trick entirely.

4. **The paralysis clause is the whole encounter, and it hinges on one hit.**
   When the ghoul connects, the Body save is the moment: Orsible (1d20-1) and Pip
   (1d20-1) are badly exposed, while Hurgo (+2) and Branna (+2) are solid. The
   fight would have read completely differently had the ghoul rolled two hits on
   a squishy. The danger is real but swingy, gated behind a to-hit roll the
   monster is unlikely to make at this level.

5. **The 2-page core was self-sufficient.** Character creation, gear, the core
   roll, saves, initiative, attacks, damage, Turn Undead, stocking, Threat, and
   XP were all on the two pages. I never had to open a supplement.

## Suggestion for the Next Run

The fight was decisive well short of 5 rounds because the stocking roll produced
a single weak monster. To actually exercise a longer, in-doubt combat on the
core rules, the next encounter wants either a monster group that comes with a
count (Goblin Band 1d6, or Orc Patrol 1d4) or a tougher solo foe read off the
deeper-level scaling. Say the word and I will carry this same party into the next
chamber and run that.

## Second Encounter: The Orc Patrol

Same party, same delve, no rest. Resources carried over: Orsible's Magic Missile
is spent (Sleep still prepared, and orcs are living, so it would bite this time),
2 oil flasks left; Branna's Cure Light Wounds unused; Hurgo and Pip fresh.

Pressing into the next chamber, the treasure room from the earlier stocking, the
party finds an **Orc Patrol** holding it.

Setup rolls:

- Patrol size 1d4 = `[3]`. Three orcs: AC 14, Atk +2 (1d8). HP rolled 1d6 per HD for variety: A `[3 6]`=9, B `[3 2]`=5, C `[4 1]`=5.
- Surprise, 1d6 each: party `[1]` (caught off guard), orcs `[6]` (not). The orcs had the drop.
- Reaction, 2d6 + CHA: `[4 5]`=9, + Branna's CHA +2 = 11, so "friendly, for now." Holding the advantage, the orcs bark a demand for a toll rather than striking.
- The party wants the chamber, not a deal, so the parley breaks. The orcs' surprise is spent on the standoff; no free round.
- Initiative, 1d6 each: party `[3]`, orcs `[1]`. Party acts first.

Orsible keeps Sleep in reserve as a panic button and throws oil while the fight
is even.

### Round 1, party first

- **Hurgo** sword vs Orc A: `[17]`+3 = 20 vs 14. Hit, 1d6+1 = `[2]`+1 = 3. Orc A 9 -> 6.
- **Branna** mace vs Orc B: **natural `[20]`**. Auto-hit, double damage: (`[3]`+1) x2 = 8. Orc B (5 HP) dead.
- **Pip** sling vs Orc C: `[11]`+3 = 14 vs 14. Hit, 1d6 = `[5]`. Orc C (5 HP) dead.

### Morale and the end

**Outcome: party victory, one effective round, no damage taken.** A crit and a
max sling roll erased two orcs before they acted, and the morale rule folded the
third.

## Updated Playtest Notes

The second fight repeated the first's shape, which is itself the finding.

6. **Morale ends small-group fights early, by design.** A patrol that loses half
   its number in a round breaks. This is a core OSR feature and it works, but it
   means a 5-to-10-round slugfest will be rare against anything that checks
   morale. A long, in-doubt fight on the core rules wants a foe that does not
   check: Mindless undead, a sworn or cornered enemy, or a single high-HP solo.

7. **The natural-20 crit is very swingy at level 1.** Branna's double-damage 20
   deleted a full 5-HP orc in one blow. With monster HP this low, a single crit
   is often a whole enemy, which compounds the alpha-strike problem: win
   initiative, land a crit, and the encounter ends before it begins.

8. **Zero damage taken, again.** Three fights' worth of monster attacks (ghoul
   twice, this patrol) and not one landed. The party reached AC 14 cheaply and
   the 2-HD foes attack at only +2, needing a 12. Winning initiative, high damage
   output, and AC the monsters cannot reliably beat make even-numbered level-1
   combat read as one-sided in the party's favor. The recurring AC question
   (armor value stacking full DEX) is the lever; worth deciding before this
   reads as the intended balance.

9. **Sleep was never needed.** Held in reserve, it would have been a second win
   button had the fight turned. A Magic-User with Sleep against any group of
   4-HD-or-less living creatures is a near-automatic encounter-ender; the party
   conserving it was a choice, not a necessity.

## Where to Take It Next

To see a drawn-out, uncertain fight on the core rules, pick a foe that will not
break and can survive the alpha strike. Both are core:

- A **Skeleton Guard** pack (1d6, Mindless, never checks morale, resistant to
  edged and piercing so swords bite for half, vulnerable to Branna's blunt mace).
  This one grinds.
- A single **deeper-level solo** scaled up: enough HP to outlast round one, and an
  attack bonus high enough to actually threaten AC 14.

## Third Encounter: Skeleton Guards

Pressing into the burial vault, the party rouses the crypt's own wardens. Chosen
as a foe that will not break (Mindless) and that punishes the wrong weapons
(resistant to edged and piercing, vulnerable to blunt).

Setup rolls:

- Count 1d6 = `[3]`. Three skeletons, HD 1, AC 13, Atk +1 (1d6). HP 1d6 each: S1 `[2]`, S2 `[6]`, S3 `[5]`.
- Surprise, 1d6 each: party `[5]`, skeletons `[4]`. Neither surprised.
- Initiative, 1d6 each: party `[2]`, skeletons `[3]`. Skeletons act first.
- No reaction or morale: Mindless undead neither parley nor break.

The party adapts to the resistances. Branna leads with the mace (blunt, double
damage). Hurgo sheaths his edged sword, which would do half, and draws the hammer
from his spike kit as an improvised blunt weapon (light 1d4, -2 to hit so +1
total, but blunt for double). Orsible's oil does full fire damage. Pip's sling
and dagger both read as piercing, so he spends his action to Aid instead.

### Round 1, skeletons first

- **Skel1** vs Hurgo: `[2]`+1 = 3 vs 14. Miss.
- **Skel3** vs Hurgo: `[13]`+1 = 14 vs 14. **Hit**, 1d6 = `[1]`. Hurgo 8 -> 7. First damage the party has taken in four fights.
- **Skel2** vs Branna: `[3]`+1 = 4 vs 14. Miss.

Party:

- **Pip** Aids Branna (+2 to her attack), forgoing his own weak piercing attack.
- **Branna** mace vs Skel2: `[8]`+3 +2 Aid = 13 vs AC 13. Hit, (1d6+1)x2 blunt = (`[6]`+1)x2 = 14. Skel2 (6) dead.
- **Hurgo** hammer vs Skel1: `[17]`+1 = 18 vs 13. Hit, (1d4+1)x2 blunt = (`[3]`+1)x2 = 8. Skel1 (2) dead.
- **Orsible** oil vs Skel3: `[18]`+1 = 19 vs 13. Hit, 1d6 fire = `[6]`. Skel3 (5) dead.

**Outcome: party victory in one round. Hurgo took 1 damage, the only hit any
monster has landed so far.**

### Aftermath and XP

- Skeletons: 3 x (25 XP x 1 HD) = **75 XP**. No treasure; these were wardens, not a stocked hoard.
- Running campaign total: 900 + 75 = **975 XP** (600 still contingent on reaching town).

### Note

This encounter was chosen as a grind and resolved in one round anyway, but the
reason is mechanical, not a dice fluke. Blunt and fire sidestep the skeletons'
resistance, so Branna and Orsible dealt double and full damage against only three
bodies. Had the party kept using the sword and sling (edged and piercing, halved
here), the same three skeletons would have taken roughly twice as long to clear.
The damage-type system is the entire fight: the skeletons are a puzzle about
weapons, and the party solved it. Pip's Aid also mattered, turning Branna's miss
into the kill, a clean use of the one core action a weak attacker always has.

## Boss Battle: The Drowned Warden

A GM-built solo, per the Bestiary "Making New Monsters" kit, deliberately tuned
to challenge four level-1 PCs: HP well above the 5-per-HD baseline, two attacks a
round, and one telegraphed trick. The deepest vault of the Sunken Crypt, half
flooded, where a bloated barnacled corpse-giant stands watch over a reliquary.

**Stat block.** HD 5, HP 40, AC 15, saves 10+HD = 15. Two slams a round, +5 each,
1d8 bludgeon. Vulnerable to fire (oil doubles). Undead: immune to Sleep, Charmed,
Poisoned, Frightened; can be Turned. Signature, telegraphed: **Undertow**, a round
after it rears and the water sucks back, a wave hits everyone in the water,
Dodge save vs 15 or 2d6 and knocked Prone and dragged toward the deep.

Party resources entering: Branna's Cure Light Wounds unspent, her Turn available,
Orsible down to 2 oil flasks (doubled here) and a dagger, Hurgo at 7/8 from the
skeletons. Sleep is dead weight against undead.

Setup: surprise 1d6 each, party `[4]`, warden `[6]`, neither surprised, so no
backstab. Initiative, 1d6 each: party `[2]`, warden `[2]`. **Tie: every round is
simultaneous, resolve everything before anyone drops.** That rule shaped the whole
fight.

### Round 1 (Warden 40)

- Party vs AC 15: Branna `[19]` hits for 1d6+1 = `[4]`+1 = 5. Hurgo `[7]`, Pip `[12]`, Orsible's oil `[13]` all miss. Warden 40 -> 35.
- Warden drives both slams at Branna (undead hate the holy symbol): `[20]` natural, doubled but a low `[1]` = 2; `[18]` hits for `[6]`. Total 8 on a 5-HP cleric. Her mace blow landed first by the tie rule, then she drops to 0.
- **Branna Brush with Death `[6]` = Close Call, full HP.** She goes under and surges back up.

### Round 2 (Warden 35)

- Hurgo `[19]` hits 6, Branna `[12]`+3 = 15 hits 4, Pip `[14]`+3 = 17 hits 4. Orsible's **last** oil `[13]` misses, both flasks wasted. Warden 35 -> 21.
- Warden: slam Hurgo `[17]` hits `[2]` (Hurgo 7 -> 5); slam Branna `[2]` misses.
- At 21, just past half, the Warden rears: **Undertow telegraph.** The wave lands next round. Pip and Orsible (5 and 3 HP) climb the stone dais to get clear; Hurgo and Branna stay in the water and gamble on killing it.

### Round 3 (Warden 21, telegraph)

- Hurgo rolls a **natural `[1]`**: fumble. His sword hits only stone and the receding current fouls his footing, no chance to brace for the wave. Branna `[12]` misses. **Zero damage.**
- Warden: slam Hurgo `[17]` hits `[1]` (Hurgo 5 -> 4); slam Branna `[3]` misses. Warden still 21.

### Round 4 (Warden 21, THE WAVE)

- Undertow crashes. Branna Dodge save `[20]` natural, avoids it clean. Hurgo, fouled by his fumble, is caught automatically: 2d6 = `[3 3]` = 6, Hurgo 4 -> 0, dragged toward the deep.
- Party attacks (all act, tie): Hurgo `[5]` misses, Branna **natural `[1]`**, Pip **natural `[1]`**, Orsible's thrown dagger `[9]` misses. **Zero damage again.** Warden still 21.
- **Hurgo Brush with Death `[6]` = Close Call, full HP.** He plants his feet in the current and comes up roaring, back to 8.

### Round 5 (Warden 21)

- Hurgo `[5]` misses yet again. Orsible climbs down to steady Branna (Aid +2): Branna `[12]`+5 = 17 hits for 7. Pip `[19]` hits for 4. Warden 21 -> 10.
- Warden: slam Hurgo `[6]` misses; slam Branna `[13]` hits for `[8]`. Branna's mace landed first, then she drops to 0 a second time.
- **Branna Brush with Death `[2]` = Maimed.** The slam takes her eye. Unconscious 1d4 turns, then 1 HP, Injured for good. The party's best damage-dealer is down and has to be kept from drowning.

### Round 6 (Warden 10)

- Orsible drops everything to haul Branna clear. Hurgo `[19]` finally connects but rolls `[1]` for 2 damage; Pip `[13]` hits for 2. Warden 10 -> 6.
- Warden: slam Hurgo `[6]` misses; slam Pip `[10]`+5 = 15 hits for `[4]`. Pip 5 -> 1.

The boss is reeling at 6 but the party is wrecked. Pip, Orsible, and the dragged
Branna withdraw toward the stair. Hurgo takes one parting swing before following.

- Hurgo `[19]` hits, 1d6+1 = `[6]`+1 = 7. **Warden 6 -> dead.**
- By the tie rule its dying fists still fall, both on Hurgo: `[10]`+5 = 15 hits for `[7]`, `[14]`+5 = 19 hits for `[2]`. Total 9. Hurgo 8 -> 0 as the Warden falls. They go down together in the black water.
- **Hurgo Brush with Death `[5]` = Shaken.** Alive at 1 HP, sword lost in the water, Injured until a Safe Haven rest.

### Outcome

**Party victory in 7 rounds, at heavy cost.** The Warden is dead. Branna is maimed
(one eye) and unconscious, Hurgo and Pip are at 1 HP, only Orsible is unhurt, and
they still have to carry two near-corpses and the loot out of a flooded crypt past
wandering-monster checks.

- Warden: 25 XP x 5 HD = **125 XP**.
- Hoard (3+ HD leader, one Treasure roll): `[4]` = Art, a silver reliquary worth **300 GP**, two slots.
- Running campaign total: 975 + 125 + 300 = **1,400 XP** (900 of it gold, contingent on reaching town), about 350 a head pooled. A strong single delve, still short of the 2,000 for Level 2.

### Note (factual, for tuning)

This fight ran long and stayed in doubt where the three sized-to-level encounters
did not, and the reasons are concrete:

- **HP survived the alpha strike.** 40 HP meant the party's best round (round 2, 14 damage) did not end it, so the monster got to act many times.
- **Two attacks at +5 could land on AC 14.** A single +2 attacker could not; doubling the attacks and raising the bonus is what let the Warden threaten the party the earlier foes never did. Two hits on one 5-HP character is a drop, which is what put Branna and later Hurgo on the death table.
- **The tied initiative amplified everything.** Simultaneous rounds meant the Warden landed its killing slams on Hurgo in the same round he killed it. A won or lost initiative would have changed who died.
- **The telegraphed wave created a decision, not just damage.** Splitting the party (squishies to the dais, blades in the water) is exactly the choice The Answer rule is meant to force, and Hurgo's fumble turning off his brace was the fiction paying out.
- **The fire lever mostly missed.** Orsible's doubled oil was the party's answer to the HP pool, and both flasks missed on AC 15. Had they landed (roughly 7 each), the fight would have been two rounds shorter. The vulnerability was the right design hook even though the dice wasted it.

Across five encounters, the single scaled solo was the only one that produced a
real fight. The lever is monster HP, attack count, and to-hit against the party's
AC, not monster count or morale.
